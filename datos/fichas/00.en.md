# History of the birthplace of Francisco de Goya y Lucientes

<!-- autoría -->
Ricardo Centellas & José Luis Ona

Zapater y Gómez was the first to try, in 1868, to identify the house where tradition held that the painter was born on 30 March 1746:

> “At the house now numbered 18 on Calle Alfóndiga, in the Lower Quarter. It belonged, along with another house in the same village, to Goya’s family, which still bears the surname Mozota. It is now owned by His Excellency the Count of Fuentes and has been turned into an inn.”[^1]

Twenty-five years later, the painter Anselmo Gascón de Gotor (1865–1927), a member of the local bourgeoisie, visited Fuendetodos with his brother Pedro at the invitation of Father Tomás Palacios, a priest born in the village. Their visit helped establish the tradition of artistic pilgrimage to Goya’s birthplace, an “artistic journey” that would become popular in later years. In 1894, Anselmo Gascón de Gotor had the distinction of being the first known person to reproduce photographs of the birthplace and of its owner, Benita Aznar Lucientes. He described her as “a relative of the Aragonese painter, in the second and fourth degrees of kinship along an unequal collateral line.” She was said to resemble Goya. Benita owned the house, kept it in her possession and showed it “to the many art lovers who wish to visit it.”[^2]

The sources agree that Goya’s birthplace was on Calle Alfóndiga, but differ over who owned it. In 1868 Zapater y Gómez identified it with the Count of Fuentes’s inn, which had apparently belonged to the Mozota Goya family.[^3]

The difference between the number given by Zapater y Gómez and the old tile still on the façade may reflect a later renumbering of the street. The discrepancy over ownership is more significant. In the mid-nineteenth century the Mozota Goya family did own an inn on Calle Alfóndiga, divided into two shares. One was registered to Jacinto Catalán, husband of Rosa Mozota Goya. The other was recorded under “Widow of Ramón Mozota”, that is Rafaela Goya, Tomás’s daughter and Francisco’s niece. The inn adjoined Mariano Benedí’s house and the vicarage garden. Benedí’s record places his house in the Plaza del Barrio Bajo, on the edge of the Alfóndiga district. The inn shared by Tomás Goya’s descendants was therefore a different building from the one registered to María Lucientes. The Count of Fuentes also owned an inn in the same district, which may have confused Zapater y Gómez. The count’s inn stood between the lower lane where María Lucientes lived and La Parada, below the parish church, in a building restored in recent years.[^4]

Twenty-five years later, in 1893, Anselmo Gascón de Gotor identified Benita Aznar Lucientes as the owner and occupant of the birthplace. Benita was still living in the same house in 1913, an important year for the building. The commemorative plaque that survives today was placed on its façade, and the painter Ignacio Zuloaga became involved. He acquired the property in 1915. Asked by a reporter from the Zaragoza newspaper *La Crónica*, the eighty-seven-year-old Benita said she had lived there since birth. “I inherited it from my parents, and they in turn inherited it from theirs,” she added.[^5]

Who was Benita Aznar Lucientes? Benita does not appear in the parish rolls before 1835, the last year for which a roll survives. If the *La Crónica* reporter’s information is correct, she would have been born around 1826.[^6] She may be the Francisca [Benita?] listed as a possible daughter of Joaquín Aznar and Inés Lucientes at house no. 63 in the 1835 roll, alongside Matea and José, the couple’s older children. At that date, only the children of Joaquín and Inés could have borne the surnames Aznar Lucientes, in that order, in Fuendetodos.[^7]

The 1846 property tax register may help identify the painter’s birthplace.[^8] One of its sheets, filed alphabetically by first name as was usual at the time, is registered to “María Lucientes”. She was almost certainly María Eduarda, born around 1798, who lived with and cared for her parents from 1824 until their deaths, Inés Rotellar in 1828 and Pascual Lucientes in 1834, in the family’s half-house on Calle Alfóndiga. Besides half a hayloft at Peirón Alto and a yard plot at La Lámpara, the register lists a small enclosure opposite her home. She inherited all four properties from her parents.[^9] Luis Soro’s sheet lists a house on Calle Alfóndiga adjoining one owned by Alejandro Salueña and the “yard of the heirs of Pasqual Lucientes”.[^10] María Lucientes’s half-house adjoined Salvador Sarto’s half and Benito Cortés’s garden. These boundaries place it where Benita Aznar Lucientes was living in 1913. A group of Aragonese artists acquired the house and offered it to Ignacio Zuloaga on 30 April 1915.[^11] It is also surely the same half-house in the “Barrio de Lafondiga” listed in the 1819 property record of Pascual Lucientes, María Eduarda’s father,[^12] and occupied by his own father, Miguel de Lucientes, until his death in 1798.[^13]

The 1788 land register records that Miguel de Lucientes, Goya’s maternal uncle, owned a “house, yard and garden”. The register gives no location, but the property can be placed in the Alfóndiga district, the only part of Fuendetodos where some houses backed onto gardens. These included the “priest’s garden”, also called the “vicarage garden”, and, by the mid-nineteenth century, Benito Cortés’s garden. These plots lay beside the built-up area and benefited from spring water. In a village with almost entirely rain-fed agriculture, such gardens were a luxury reserved for the wealthiest residents.

During his father Miguel’s lifetime, Pascual Lucientes received half a house and a yard from the family estate. This need not have involved physically dividing the building. The father and his son’s family could simply have agreed to share the rooms differently.[^14] The 1794 parish rolls record Miguel Lucientes y Salvador living in the house on Calle Alfóndiga with his wife, Mariana Lahora.[^15]

This was probably the same building that had belonged years earlier to Miguel de Lucientes senior, known from a notarial record dated just two years after Goya’s birth in 1746. The record gives the general location as “Barrio Baxo” (Lower Quarter) without specifying Calle Alfóndiga. It does, however, mention a garden adjoining the “Cerrada del Lugar” and Miguel de Lucientes’s house and yard. This suggests that it was the same house on Calle Alfóndiga, long owned by the Lucientes, where Benita Aznar Lucientes lived and where the commemorative plaque was placed in 1913.[^16]
